# Start here

This is the front door of a free, open, five-year university curriculum for computer science and IT. It takes a complete beginner to job-ready practice in one of 20 majors, and on to research. Everything is free to read, and the course outlines may be reused under the [MIT license](../LICENSE).

Read this page first. It takes about 15 minutes, and at the end you will know where to begin and what to do this week.

## Who this university is for

| You are… | What this gives you | Where you start |
|---|---|---|
| **A complete beginner** (school maths, no programming) | A full path from zero: Year 1 assumes nothing beyond school arithmetic and algebra | [Year 1](../01_year_1_foundations/README.md), course FND101 |
| **A college student** in a BTech, BE, BCA, BSc, MCA or similar | A deeper, more practical version of your syllabus, with labs and projects your college may not set | The [placement check](05_placement_check.md), then the courses you cannot yet pass |
| **A working professional** changing role (for example, support to cloud, testing to software engineering, analyst to data engineer) | A way to fill exactly the gaps between your current job and your target role | The [placement check](05_placement_check.md), then your target major's fast track |
| **A self-taught developer** with projects but gaps in theory | The fundamentals interviews and senior work still test: algorithms, systems, databases, networks | Year 2 diagnostics in the [placement check](05_placement_check.md) |
| **A teacher, mentor or student club** | Ready course outlines: weekly plans, labs, projects, assessments | Any course README; see [how this university works](01_how_this_university_works.md) |

It is **not** an accredited degree. Nobody grades you here, and no certificate is issued. What you get is a structured path and a portfolio of evidence that employers, clients and graduate schools can inspect. [The FAQ](06_faq.md) explains what that can and cannot replace.

## How to use it in 15 minutes

1. **Minutes 0–3. Understand the shape.** Five years, ten semesters. Years 1–2 are the same for everyone. In Years 3–5 you follow one of 20 majors, add one overlap specialization in Year 4, and finish with research and a thesis or capstone. Each course is 4 credits, about 120 hours of work.
2. **Minutes 3–6. Understand how progress is measured.** Every course ends in a **mastery gate**: a concrete task you must be able to do (for example, "find the commit that broke the tests with `git bisect` and fix it, with AI off"), not a percentage. Levels run from L0 (absolute beginner) to L5 (research). Details are in [how this university works](01_how_this_university_works.md).
3. **Minutes 6–10. Find your starting point.** Open the [placement check](05_placement_check.md) and try the five questions for any course you think you already know. If you answer all five without help, attempt that course's mastery gate; if you pass, skip the course.
4. **Minutes 10–13. Look at the majors.** Skim the comparison table in [choose your major](02_choose_your_major.md). You do not have to choose now; most people decide during Year 2. Note two or three that interest you.
5. **Minutes 13–15. Plan this week.** Pick your pace (full-time, about 40–48 hours a week, or part-time, about 15–25), block the hours in your calendar, and set up your machine with [set up your lab](04_set_up_your_lab.md).

## Your first week

- [ ] Read [how this university works](01_how_this_university_works.md) and [the AI-native study protocol](03_ai_native_study_protocol.md).
- [ ] Set up your machine ([set up your lab](04_set_up_your_lab.md)) and create a GitHub account with two-factor authentication.
- [ ] Create a public repository called, for example, `cs-university-progress` with a `PROGRESS.md` file (a template is in [how this university works](01_how_this_university_works.md#5-self-certifying-your-progress)).
- [ ] Take the AI-off baseline: the placement questions for FND101, FND103 and FND104. Record your score and the date, even if it is zero.
- [ ] Open [FND101](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND101_programming_i_computational_thinking_with_python/README.md) and do week 1.
- [ ] Find one study partner, or join a group, and agree a weekly check-in time.

## Ground rules that apply everywhere

1. **Evidence over attendance.** You move on when you can do the gate task, not when you have read the chapters.
2. **AI is a tool you must be able to check.** Every course says what to do with AI, what to do without it, and how to verify it. Exams and mastery gates are AI-off unless the course says otherwise.
3. **Security is learned legally.** Practise only on systems you own or on platforms built for practice, with written permission for anything else.
4. **Facts carry sources and dates.** Market numbers, prices and exam details change. Every page shows when it was checked; check again before you spend money.
