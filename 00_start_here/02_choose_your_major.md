# Choose your major

| Read time | Decide by | Uses data from |
|---|---|---|
| ~30 minutes (plus a 4-week taste test) | The end of Year 2 | [Market map](../06_career_and_market_readiness/01_market_map/README.md) and [AI impact matrix](../06_career_and_market_readiness/01_market_map/ai_impact_matrix.md), checked October 2026 |

You declare a major at the end of Year 2. Years 1–2 are the same for everyone, so you do not need to choose on day one, and the choice is less permanent than it feels: switching mostly costs the new major's entry courses. This page gives you a short quiz to build a shortlist, a comparison of all 20 majors, a way to test your shortlist cheaply, and the rules for switching and combining majors.

Market facts below come from the market map and its role pages, which link every number to a dated source. Lines marked **Analysis** are interpretation, not measurement.

## 1. The quiz: build a shortlist

For each question, pick every answer that is true for you (you may pick more than one). Each answer lists majors; put a tick next to each major listed. After the ten questions, the three or four majors with the most ticks are your shortlist. The quiz cannot tell you what you will love; it tells you where to look first.

**Q1. Which problem would you happily think about on a Sunday?**
- (a) Why a program is slow, or how a system stays correct when machines fail → CSG, BE, OPS, DEN
- (b) How to make an app or page feel fast, clear and pleasant → FE, MOB
- (c) What the numbers say about why customers left → DAN, DSC
- (d) How an attacker could get in, and how to stop them → SEC, SAR, OFF
- (e) How a model learns, or why it fails → MLE, DLR, AIE
- (f) How to make a physical device sense and act → EMB
- (g) How to make a messy real organisation's process work with software → FDE, NET, AIE

**Q2. What kind of output do you most enjoy producing?**
- (a) Code other engineers build on (libraries, APIs, platforms) → SWE, BE, OPS, CLD
- (b) Something a non-technical person sees and uses → FE, MOB, FDE
- (c) A clear answer, chart or recommendation → DAN, DSC
- (d) A proof, a measurement, a paper → CSG, DLR
- (e) A test suite or evaluation that catches what others missed → QAE, AIE, SEC
- (f) A working network, server or device fleet → NET, EMB, CLD

**Q3. Which school subjects or Year 1 courses felt easiest?**
- (a) Mathematics, especially proofs ([FND103](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND103_discrete_mathematics_for_computing/README.md)) → CSG, DLR, SAR
- (b) Linear algebra, calculus and statistics ([FND113](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND113_linear_algebra_and_calculus_for_computing/README.md), [FND116](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND116_probability_and_statistics_i/README.md)) → DSC, MLE, DLR, DAN
- (c) Programming and data structures ([FND101](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND101_programming_i_computational_thinking_with_python/README.md), [FND112](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND112_data_structures_and_algorithms_i/README.md)) → SWE, BE, AIE
- (d) Hardware, bits and the operating system ([FND102](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND102_how_computers_work_bits_hardware/README.md)) → EMB, NET, OPS
- (e) Linux, shell and Git ([FND104](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND104_the_developers_toolkit_linux_shell_git/README.md)) → OPS, CLD, NET
- (f) Web pages and design ([FND114](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND114_web_foundations_http_html_css_and_javascript/README.md)) → FE, MOB
- (g) SQL and data modelling ([FND115](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND115_databases_i_data_modeling_and_sql/README.md)) → DAN, DEN, BE
- (h) Writing and presenting ([FND106](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND106_technical_communication_and_professional_skills/README.md)) → FDE, SAR, DAN

**Q4. How do you feel about being on call for a system that breaks at 3 a.m.?**
- (a) Fine, I like fixing things under pressure → OPS, SEC, BE, CLD, NET
- (b) Acceptable occasionally → SWE, DEN, MLE, AIE, MOB, FE
- (c) I would rather not → CSG, DSC, DAN, QAE, DLR

**Q5. How much do you want to talk to customers or non-engineers?**
- (a) Every day; I like it → FDE, DAN, SAR
- (b) Sometimes → SWE, AIE, DSC, QAE, MOB, FE, NET
- (c) Rarely; I prefer deep solo work → CSG, DLR, EMB, DEN, OFF

**Q6. Which risk profile suits you right now?** (See section 3.)
- (a) I need a job within about a year of finishing the core; I prefer roles with many entry openings → SWE, BE, QAE, CLD, DEN, NET, DAN
- (b) I can invest longer for a field with strong growth but a harder or more competitive entry → AIE, MLE, SEC, OPS, FDE, EMB, DSC
- (c) I am aiming for research or a specialist role and accept a long, competitive path (possibly a master's or PhD) → CSG, DLR, SAR, OFF

**Q7. Which of these would you read for fun?**
- (a) An outage postmortem from a large company → OPS, BE, SWE, NET
- (b) A paper on a new model architecture → DLR, MLE, CSG
- (c) A breach report or a CVE write-up → SEC, SAR, OFF
- (d) A teardown of a well-designed app → FE, MOB
- (e) A case study of a company using data to change a decision → DAN, DSC, FDE
- (f) A datasheet for a sensor or microcontroller → EMB
- (g) The release notes of an AI agent framework → AIE, FDE

**Q8. How do you like to verify that your work is right?**
- (a) Proofs and formal reasoning → CSG, SAR
- (b) Tests, many of them → QAE, SWE, BE
- (c) Experiments and statistics → DSC, DAN, MLE, DLR
- (d) Running it on real hardware or a real network → EMB, NET
- (e) Dashboards and alerts in production → OPS, CLD, SEC
- (f) Evaluation sets and human review → AIE, QAE

**Q9. What does your budget for hardware look like?**
- (a) Laptop only → any major (cloud free tiers and notebooks cover most labs; see [set up your lab](04_set_up_your_lab.md))
- (b) I can buy a microcontroller kit and a logic analyser over time → EMB
- (c) I may need cloud GPU time for some projects → DLR, MLE (most labs still fit free notebooks)

**Q10. Which sentence fits you best?**
- (a) "I want to understand everything underneath." → CSG, EMB, OPS
- (b) "I want to build products people use." → SWE, FE, MOB, AIE
- (c) "I want to be the person who knows if it is safe." → SEC, SAR, QAE, OFF
- (d) "I want to be the person who knows what the data means." → DAN, DSC, DEN
- (e) "I want to make AI actually work in the real world." → AIE, MLE, FDE
- (f) "I want to keep a company's systems running." → NET, CLD, OPS

## 3. What the market data says, and how to use it

From the [market map](../06_career_and_market_readiness/01_market_map/README.md) (all checked October 2026):

- **Growth is concentrated.** The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects data scientists +35%, computer and information research scientists +22% and information security analysts +21% for 2025–35, against −7% for computer programmers and −4% for network and systems administrators ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/home.htm)).
- **Big families still hire most people.** Software developers: 1,717,800 US jobs and +10%. A slower-growing large field can hire more people than a fast-growing small one.
- **Employers worldwide expect growth** in big data, FinTech, AI and machine learning, security management, data warehousing and DevOps roles ([WEF Future of Jobs 2025](https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf)). In India, the fastest-growing roles employers expect are big data specialists, AI and ML specialists and security management specialists (same source).
- **India is shifting from headcount to skill mix.** NASSCOM expects FY27 hiring "to shift from volume to skill mix" ([NASSCOM 2026](https://nasscom.in/knowledge-center/publications/technology-sector-india-strategic-review-2026)).
- **The entry rung is under the most pressure** in families where AI automates the tasks juniors used to do ([Canaries in the Coal Mine, Nov 2025](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2025/12/CanariesintheCoalMine_Nov25.pdf)).

**Analysis: how to turn this into a decision.**

1. **Use the "still human-led" column of the [AI impact matrix](../06_career_and_market_readiness/01_market_map/ai_impact_matrix.md) as a job description of your future.** If those tasks (for example "incident command", "metric definitions", "real-time guarantees") sound dull to you, the major will feel dull in five years.
2. **Match risk to your situation, not to headlines.** If you need income soon, pick a family with many entry titles and build the skills that move you up (automation, cloud, testing depth). If you can wait, a more competitive field with strong growth can pay off, but only if you build public evidence early.
3. **Do not choose by salary medians alone.** The market map shows two Indian software salary sources that differ about four times because they measure different employers and different kinds of pay. Your pay will depend more on where you work and how deep you go than on the major's name.
4. **Re-check twice a year.** The market map lists when each source updates.

## 4. Test your shortlist before you commit: the 4-week taste test

Reading about a field is weak evidence. Doing a small piece of real work in it is strong evidence. Spend about 5 hours a week for 4 weeks (alongside Year 2, or before it) on one task per shortlisted major:

| Major | A taste task (L1–L2, from existing course projects) |
|---|---|
| CSG | Prove a small algorithm correct and confirm it with a brute-force checker ([CS201](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_3/CS201_algorithms_and_complexity/README.md) labs), or build the JSON parser that must survive a conformance suite ([CS216](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_4/CS216_programming_languages_compilers_and_interpreters/README.md) P1) |
| SWE | Review a real open-source pull request and land one small change of your own |
| BE | Build "exactly-once payment effects over an unreliable network" ([CS212](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_4/CS212_distributed_systems_i/README.md) P1) |
| FE | Build the accessible personal portfolio ([FND114](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND114_web_foundations_http_html_css_and_javascript/README.md) P1), then measure and improve its Core Web Vitals |
| MOB | Ship a two-screen app to your own phone with offline storage |
| CLD / OPS | Containerize, test and ship a three-tier app ([CS215](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_4/CS215_cloud_and_devops_foundations/README.md) P1) on a free tier with a budget alarm |
| SEC / SAR / OFF | Threat-model a small app with STRIDE and complete ten [PortSwigger Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security) labs ([CS213](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_4/CS213_security_foundations/README.md)) |
| DAN / DSC | Answer a real question with a public dataset: SQL, one chart, a confidence interval, a one-page write-up ([FND115](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND115_databases_i_data_modeling_and_sql/README.md), [FND116](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_2/FND116_probability_and_statistics_i/README.md)) |
| DEN | Build a small pipeline that loads a public dataset daily into a warehouse table, with a data-quality check |
| MLE / DLR | Build "a leakage-proof model tied to a real decision" ([CS214](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_4/CS214_machine_learning_foundations/README.md) P2), starting from a baseline |
| AIE / FDE | Build cited answers over a document set with an evaluation of 30 cases that shows accuracy and one failure ([FND105](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND105_ai_native_learning_and_building/README.md) P2); for FDE, interview one non-technical person about their workflow first |
| EMB | Finish nand2tetris projects 1–5 ([FND102](../01_year_1_foundations/semester_1/FND102_how_computers_work_bits_hardware/README.md)) or blink and read a sensor on a low-cost microcontroller board |
| QAE | Write a test plan using boundary values and decision tables for a small app, then automate five of the cases |
| NET | Build a home lab network (or a simulated one) with two subnets, a firewall rule and DNS, and capture the traffic with Wireshark ([CS204](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/semester_3/CS204_computer_networks/README.md)) |

After each task, write three lines: what you enjoyed, what you dreaded, and what you would want to learn next. Choose the major where the third line is longest.

## 5. Declaring

Follow the declaration steps in the [Year 2 README](../02_year_2_core_computer_science/README.md#declaring-your-major): check the entry requirements (each is a Year 1 or Year 2 course gate), write a one-page declaration with your target roles, your major, a possible overlap and your evidence, and review it with a mentor or peer.

## 6. Switching majors

Switching is normal. Because Years 1–2 are shared, the cost of switching depends only on when you switch and how close the two majors are.

| When you switch | What it costs |
|---|---|
| Before Year 3 starts | Nothing beyond the new major's entry requirements (all Year 1–2 courses) |
| During Year 3 | The new major's Year 3 courses you have not taken. Courses you already passed can count as electives |
| During Year 4 or later | The new major's Year 3 core, possibly a semester or two. Consider an **overlap** instead (section 7) |

Common switches that cost little, because the majors share skills (from the role pages' "adjacent roles" sections):

| From | To | Why it is a short move | Bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| QAE | SWE | Test automation engineers already write production-grade code | SWE302, SWE304 |
| NET | CLD or OPS | Networking and Linux are the base of cloud and operations work; "the most common upward move" for this family | CLD304, OPS302 |
| DAN | DSC or DEN | Same SQL and data; add modelling and causal inference (DSC) or pipelines (DEN) | DSC301, DEN301; overlap OV06 |
| BE | CLD, DEN or AIE | Same storage, messaging and API skills | Overlaps OV02, OV06, OV08 |
| FE | MOB or BE | React Native or Flutter are close to web skills; full-stack adds APIs and data | MOB303, BE301 |
| MLE | AIE or DSC | Same evaluation and modelling foundations | AIE301, DSC304 |
| SEC | SAR | Architecture usually comes after years of defensive work | SAR301, SAR302 |
| SWE | FDE | Add customer discovery and integration | FDE301, FDE302 |

## 8. If you still cannot decide

- **Pick the broader major** of the two you are torn between: SWE over FE, BE over DEN, MLE over DLR, SEC over SAR. You can specialise later; it is harder to widen.
- **Pick the one whose Year 3 projects you would show an interviewer with pride.** Open both majors' Year 3 `projects.md` files and read the P3 descriptions.
- **Talk to two people in each role.** Ask what they did last Tuesday, hour by hour. Compare that to the day you want.
