# DAN303 · Labs and projects

Every chart, dashboard and memo must be traceable: each number links to the query or file that produced it, every chart states its source and "data as of" date, and every visual passes a contrast check and a colour-blindness simulation. Charts in code live in a Git repository; BI files are exported and versioned (Power BI `.pbip` projects, Tableau workbooks, Metabase or Superset exports).

## Labs

| Lab | Goal | You submit | Time |

## Projects

### P1 · Chart makeover portfolio (L1, ~6 hours)

**Real-world inspiration.** Government reports, annual reports and news graphics in India and elsewhere often use 3D pies, dual axes and unnormalised maps. Datawrapper Academy and the FT Visual Vocabulary show how professional graphics desks redesign them.

**What to build.** Five makeovers of real published charts (government reports, company annual reports or news articles) whose underlying data is public, for example from the Open Government Data Platform India, RBI's Handbook of Statistics on Indian Economy or Our World in Data. For each: the original (with link), your redesign built in code, and a 150-word rationale.

**Acceptance criteria**
- Each redesign is generated by a script from the source data; no manual edits after export.
- Each rationale names the specific principle (perception ranking, baseline, normalisation, colour, labelling) and the reader's question the new chart answers.
- Every chart has a title stating the finding, a source line and units; all pass WCAG 2.2 contrast for text (4.5:1) and graphics (3:1).
- At least one makeover adds uncertainty or a denominator that the original hid.
- A reader test: 3 people answer one question from the original and from your version; record accuracy and time.

**Stretch goals.** Publish the portfolio as a static site; add a Hindi or other Indian-language version of one chart with correct number formatting.

**What it proves to an employer.** You can see what is wrong with a chart quickly and fix it with reasons, not taste.

### P2 · Regional performance dashboard, built twice (L2, ~15 hours)

**Real-world inspiration.** Regional sales and operations dashboards are the most common BI deliverable in Indian retail, FMCG and fintech firms, and the most common source of "my number does not match yours" arguments.

**What to build.** A regional performance dashboard on TheLook or Olist data for a regional-manager audience. Primary build in Power BI Desktop on a star schema with DAX measures; second build in Metabase or Looker Studio. Include a KPI row with comparisons, a trend page, a drill-down page, and a definitions page.

**Acceptance criteria**
- At least 10 DAX measures, each matching an independent SQL result to the unit on 3 filter combinations (documented in a reconciliation table).
- Indian fiscal-year YTD and same-period-last-year measures work on a continuous date table.
- Row-level security: a test user for each of 2 regions sees only that region's data.
- Every visual loads in under 3 seconds in Power BI's Performance Analyzer on the full dataset.
- The second-tool build shows the same KPI values for the same filters.
- Definitions page lists each metric's formula, grain, owner and refresh time; the "data as of" timestamp appears on every page.
- Accessibility audit passes (Lab 4 checklist).

**Stretch goals.** Use Copilot in Power BI (if you have Fabric capacity) to generate a page and document every correction; add a Tableau Pulse-style metric with an automated "what changed" note written by you from the data.

**What it proves to an employer.** You can deliver a dashboard that is correct, fast, secure and consistent across tools, which is the core of most BI analyst roles.

### P3 · Public-data story and executive briefing (L3, portfolio-grade, ~30 hours)

**Real-world inspiration.** Policy and business decisions in India draw on large public surveys and statistics: the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-6, 2023–24 fact sheets, released in May 2026 according to the NFHS website), RBI's Database on Indian Economy, and MoSPI's surveys. Turning these into a clear, honest argument is a skill policy teams, consultancies and newsrooms hire for.

**What to build.** Pick one question that matters to a named (fictional) decision-maker, for example a state health secretary choosing districts for an anaemia programme, or a bank's regional head choosing where to expand. Using NFHS-6 state and district fact sheets, RBI DBIE, MoSPI data or data.gov.in, deliver:
1. A reproducible data pipeline (scripts plus a data dictionary).
2. An interactive explainer (Evidence, Quarto or Observable) of 5–8 charts that builds one argument.
3. A one-page decision memo, answer first, with a recommendation and its uncertainty.
4. A 5-minute recorded presentation and a live 10-minute Q&A with a panel.
5. A short operational dashboard the decision-maker's team could use to track progress.

**Acceptance criteria**
- Every number in the memo and explainer is traceable to a script and a source file with a retrieval date.
- Rates are normalised (per capita or per 1,000), maps use official boundaries, and survey-based estimates are labelled as estimates with any published confidence information or a stated limitation.
- The argument survives a sceptic's review: at least 3 alternative explanations are addressed in the memo or appendix.
- A cold reader test with 3 people: after 2 minutes with the memo, at least 2 of 3 can state the recommendation and the main reason.
- The explainer passes the accessibility checklist and works at phone width.
- Q&A: you trace any challenged number to its source within 2 minutes.

**Stretch goals.** Compare NFHS-5 and NFHS-6 for the same districts, with a slopegraph and a note on changes in district boundaries; publish the explainer publicly.

**What it proves to an employer.** You can find the decision inside a large public dataset, argue it honestly with charts and words, and hold your ground under questioning, which is what separates an analyst from a report builder.
