# India company basics for founders and freelancers

| Goal | Time | Level | Back to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Choose a legal structure, understand Startup India recognition, GST registration and invoicing, receive payments from abroad correctly, and meet data-protection duties | ~4 hours to read; set-up takes days to weeks | L3 | [Founder hub](README.md) |

> **This is not legal or tax advice.** It is a map of the rules as checked in October 2026, with official sources, so you can ask a chartered accountant (CA), company secretary (CS) or lawyer the right questions. Laws, thresholds and forms change; India's Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026 and renumbered many sections, and some labour-code and data-protection rules were still being phased in during 2026. Confirm every point before you act.

## 1. Choosing a structure

| | Sole proprietorship | LLP | Private limited company | One person company (OPC) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Law | No separate statute; you trade in your own name | Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 | Companies Act, 2013 | Companies Act, 2013 |
| Separate legal entity | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Liability | Unlimited (your personal assets) | Limited to agreed contribution | Limited to share capital | Limited to share capital |
| People needed | You | At least 2 designated partners, at least one resident in India | At least 2 directors (at least one resident in India) and 2 shareholders | 1 member and a nominee |
| Set-up | Bank account, GST or other registrations as needed | Online incorporation with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) | Online incorporation via the MCA's SPICe+ form | Online incorporation via SPICe+ |
| Raising equity, ESOPs | Not possible | No shares, so no ESOPs; investors rarely invest in LLPs | Yes; the usual choice for venture funding | Limited; usually converted to a private company to raise funds |
| Ongoing compliance | Lowest: income tax, GST if registered | Annual return and statement of accounts; audit only above turnover or contribution thresholds | Highest: statutory audit every year, annual filings, board meetings | Lighter than a private company, but still a company (audit, filings) |
| DPIIT Startup India recognition | Not eligible | Eligible | Eligible | Check; the Startup India page lists private limited companies, partnership firms, LLPs and cooperative societies |

**Rules of thumb (analysis).**

- **Freelancing or testing an idea with your own money:** start as a sole proprietor; it costs little and you can incorporate later.
- **Two or more founders, no plan to raise equity, want limited liability:** LLP.
- **Plan to raise investment, issue ESOPs, or sell to large enterprise customers that prefer companies:** private limited company.
- **Alone but want a company with limited liability:** OPC, accepting more compliance than a proprietorship.

**OPC changes in 2021.** From 1 April 2021, amendments to the Companies (Incorporation) Rules allowed non-resident Indians to form OPCs, reduced the residency test for the member from 182 days to 120 days, and removed the paid-up capital (₹50 lakh) and turnover (₹2 crore) limits that used to force conversion into a private company ([SCC Online summary, Feb 2021](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2021/02/03/mca-amends-one-person-companies-opcs-rules/)).

## 2. Sole proprietorship in practice

There is no single registration that "creates" a proprietorship. You prove the business exists through registrations such as:

- **GST registration**, when required or chosen (section 5).
- **Udyam registration** as a micro enterprise, free on the government portal ([udyamregistration.gov.in](https://udyamregistration.gov.in/)); useful for delayed-payment protections (section 10).
- **Shops and establishments registration** under your state's law, where applicable.
- A **current account** in the business name, which banks open on the basis of such registrations.

Income is taxed as your personal income (section 9).

## 3. LLPs and companies: what incorporation involves

1. **Digital signature certificates (DSC)** for the designated partners or directors.
2. **Name reservation** through the MCA portal.
3. **Incorporation forms:** for companies, the integrated SPICe+ web form (which also applies for PAN, TAN and some other registrations); for LLPs, the MCA's LLP incorporation form.
4. **Constitution documents:** an LLP agreement; or a company's memorandum and articles of association.
5. **After incorporation:** bank account, initial capital, statutory registers, auditor appointment (companies), GST and other registrations as needed.

Government fees and state stamp duty depend on capital and state; check the MCA fee schedule. Professional fees for a CA or CS to handle incorporation vary widely; get two quotes.

**Annual compliance, roughly:**

| | LLP | Private limited company / OPC |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts audit | Required if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh (LLP Rules) | Required every year, regardless of size |
| Annual filings with the MCA | Annual return and statement of accounts and solvency | Financial statements and annual return |
| Income tax return | Yes | Yes |
| Meetings | — | Board meetings and an annual general meeting (OPCs and small companies have relaxations) |

Missing MCA deadlines brings per-day late fees, and repeated defaults can disqualify directors. Put the dates in a calendar on day one.

## 4. Startup India recognition (DPIIT)

Recognition by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) is free and unlocks some benefits. As listed on the Startup India portal (checked October 2026):

| Criterion | Rule |
|---|---|
| Entity type | Private limited company, partnership firm, LLP or cooperative society |
| Age | Up to 10 years from incorporation (20 years for deep-tech startups) |
| Turnover | Less than ₹200 crore in every financial year since incorporation (₹300 crore for deep-tech startups) |
| Purpose | Working towards innovation or improvement of products, services or processes, with potential for employment or wealth creation |
| Exclusion | Not formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business |
| How to apply | Through the National Single Window System (nsws.gov.in): add "Registration as a Startup" under central approvals. No fee |

**Benefits mentioned on the portal** include the income-tax holiday under section 80-IAC (a deduction for 3 consecutive years out of the first 10, for private limited companies and LLPs incorporated after 1 April 2016, after a separate approval) and the earlier "angel tax" exemption; the angel tax provision itself was abolished for all classes of investors in the Union Budget of July 2024. Recognition can also matter for ESOP tax deferral for employees ([offers: ESOP taxes](../07_job_search_offers_negotiation/offers_and_negotiation.md#32-taxes-in-india-check-current-rules)).

Source: [Startup India recognition page](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html).

## 5. GST

**When you must register.** Registration is required when your **aggregate turnover** in a financial year exceeds:

| Supplier | Normal states | Special category states |
|---|---|---|
| Services (most software and IT work) | ₹20 lakh | ₹10 lakh |
| Goods only (in states that opted for the higher limit) | ₹40 lakh | ₹20 lakh |

These thresholds were set by the GST Council at its 32nd meeting in January 2019 and took effect from 1 April 2019 ([GST Council press release](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-10/32nd_gst_press_information_bureau.pdf)). Check the current list of special category states and any changes on the [GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in/).

Key points:

- **Aggregate turnover includes exports** and exempt supplies, so a freelancer earning mostly from foreign clients still counts that income towards the threshold.
- **Some suppliers must register regardless of turnover** (for example, certain e-commerce and inter-state goods cases). Service providers below the threshold have been exempted from compulsory registration for inter-state supplies; confirm your case under section 24 of the CGST Act and the related notifications.
- **Voluntary registration** is allowed. It lets you claim input tax credit on GST you pay for business purchases, and some business clients prefer registered suppliers. It also brings monthly or quarterly returns; small taxpayers can choose a quarterly return scheme.
- **Rate.** Most IT and software services are taxed at 18%; check the current rate notification for your service code (SAC) after the 2025 rate changes.

**Exports of services.** A supply of services counts as an export when, among other conditions, the supplier is in India, the recipient is outside India, the place of supply is outside India, payment is received in convertible foreign exchange (or in rupees where the RBI permits), and the two are not merely establishments of the same person (section 2(6) of the IGST Act). Exports are **zero-rated**: you either supply under a **Letter of Undertaking (LUT)** filed online on the GST portal (form RFD-11, once per financial year) without paying IGST, or pay IGST and claim a refund. Most freelancers and small exporters use the LUT route.

## 6. Invoicing

**A GST tax invoice** must include, among other things ([CGST Rules, rule 46](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/)):

- Your name, address and GSTIN.
- A unique, consecutive invoice number for the financial year (up to 16 characters) and the date.
- The customer's name and address, and their GSTIN if registered.
- The service accounting code (SAC) and a description of the service.
- Taxable value, tax rate and amount: CGST and SGST for a customer in your state; IGST for another state; place of supply (with the state name).
- Your signature or digital signature.

**An export invoice** under LUT should also carry the endorsement "supply meant for export under bond or letter of undertaking without payment of integrated tax", show the currency, and include the client's foreign address.

**If you are not registered for GST**, do not charge GST. Issue a simple invoice with your name, address, PAN, invoice number and date, client details, description, amount and payment details.

Template (illustrative, registered supplier exporting under LUT):

```text
INVOICE  No. AR/2026-27/014         Date: 10 Oct 2026
From: Asha Rao (proprietor), Pune, Maharashtra · GSTIN 27XXXXX0000X1Z5 · PAN XXXXX0000X
To:   Example Inc., 100 Main St, Austin, TX 78701, USA
SAC 998314  Backend development: rate-limiter integration (Sep 2026), 40 hours × USD 40
Amount: USD 1,600.00     IGST: nil
SUPPLY MEANT FOR EXPORT UNDER BOND OR LETTER OF UNDERTAKING WITHOUT PAYMENT OF INTEGRATED TAX
LUT ARN: AD27XXXXXXXXXXX    Place of supply: outside India
Bank: <account name, number, SWIFT, bank address>
```

Large businesses must issue e-invoices through the government's invoice registration portal once their turnover crosses the notified threshold; this rarely applies to a new business but check it as you grow.

## 7. Receiving foreign payments

**How money arrives.** Foreign clients pay through banks (SWIFT transfers to your account) or through payment platforms that pay out to your Indian bank account. Under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), your bank (an authorised dealer) handles the conversion and asks for the **purpose** of the remittance, using RBI purpose codes; for software and IT services, choose the code your bank lists for software services and keep it consistent.

**Keep the paperwork.** For each inward remittance, get the bank's **Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (FIRA)** or certificate. You need it to show that export proceeds were received in foreign exchange (relevant for GST export treatment) and for your records.

**Platforms.** Compare the exchange-rate markup (often a bigger cost than the visible fee), payout speed, and whether the platform provides FIRA-equivalent documentation. Notes:

- Stripe describes new Indian accounts as invite-only ([Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-accounts-are-invite-only-in-india), checked October 2026).
- Merchant-of-record providers handle foreign sales tax for software sales ([pricing](pricing_and_distribution.md#6-collecting-payments)).
- PayPal, Payoneer, Wise and India-focused cross-border collection services are common for freelancers; read their current terms for India.

**US clients and tax forms.** US clients usually ask a foreign contractor for IRS Form W-8BEN, which certifies that you are not a US person and can claim treaty benefits ([IRS, About Form W-8BEN](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-8-ben)). Indian residents are taxed in India on worldwide income.

## 8. Data protection

India's **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** applies to processing of digital personal data. The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with a phased start: the Data Protection Board immediately, consent-manager provisions from November 2026, and most obligations for businesses ("data fiduciaries") from **May 2027** (about 18 months after notification), including notice and consent, security safeguards, breach notification, children's data and data-principal rights ([S.S. Rana & Co. summary](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-notifies-final-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025/); check the official notification on the [MeitY website](https://www.meity.gov.in/)).

What to build into an MVP now, so you are not retrofitting later:

| Duty | Practical step |
|---|---|
| Notice and consent | A plain-language privacy notice; consent for each purpose; easy withdrawal |
| Purpose and minimisation | Collect only what the product needs; delete when no longer needed |
| Security safeguards | Encryption in transit and at rest, access control, logging, backups ([MVP checklist](idea_to_mvp.md#5-what-you-must-verify-yourself)) |
| Breach response | A written plan: who decides, who notifies the Board and affected users, how fast |
| Children's data | Under the Act, a "child" is anyone under 18; processing their data needs verifiable parental consent. Avoid targeting children unless you are ready for this |
| Users' rights | A way to access, correct and delete their data, and a contact for grievances |

Penalties under the Act are large (up to ₹250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards). Separately, CERT-In's directions of April 2022 require specified cybersecurity incidents to be reported to CERT-In within six hours of noticing them ([CERT-In](https://www.cert-in.org.in/)). If you serve EU users, the EU's GDPR also applies.

## 9. Income tax for freelancers and small businesses

- **Proprietors and freelancers** pay tax on business or professional income at individual slab rates. Keep accounts, receipts and bank statements.
- **Presumptive taxation** for specified professionals lets eligible individuals declare 50% of gross receipts as income without detailed books, up to a receipts limit (reported as ₹50 lakh, or ₹75 lakh if cash receipts are no more than 5%). It was section 44ADA of the 1961 Act and is reported as section 58 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 ([commentary](https://caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/presumptive-taxation-for-professionals-the-50-rule-remains-but-what-changes-for-deductions-and-losses-under-the-new-tax-act)). Whether software work counts as a "specified profession" depends on its nature; ask a CA.
- **Advance tax.** Without an employer deducting tax, you pay advance tax in instalments during the year (historically by 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March). Interest applies on shortfalls.
- **TDS.** Indian business clients may deduct tax at source from your fees. Check that it appears in your Annual Information Statement and claim credit in your return.
- **LLPs and companies** are taxed as entities at their own rates; founders are taxed separately on salary or dividends.

## 10. Other registrations and protections

| Item | Why it matters | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Udyam (MSME) registration | Free; under the MSMED Act, buyers must pay registered micro and small enterprises within the agreed period, not exceeding 45 days, or owe compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate; complaints can be filed on the MSME Samadhaan portal | [udyamregistration.gov.in](https://udyamregistration.gov.in/), [samadhaan.msme.gov.in](https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/) |
| Shops and establishments | State law; may apply to offices and sometimes home businesses | Your state labour department |
| Professional tax | State-level tax on professions and employment (capped at ₹2,500 a year by the Constitution) | Your state's tax department |
| Trademark | Protects your product name; startups and small entities get reduced fees | [ipindia.gov.in](https://ipindia.gov.in/) |
| Contracts | Client agreements, founders' agreement, IP assignment | [Freelancing: contracts](freelancing_and_consulting.md#4-contracts) |
