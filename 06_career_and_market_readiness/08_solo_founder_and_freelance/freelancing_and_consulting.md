# Freelancing and consulting

| Goal | Time | Level | Back to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find clients, set rates that cover your real costs, write contracts that protect both sides, scope work, stop scope creep and get paid on time | ~5 hours to set up; then per client | L3 → L4 | [Founder hub](README.md) |

> **Not legal or tax advice.** Contract clauses below are a checklist, not a template to sign as-is. For contracts of significant value, have a lawyer review them. Tax and registration rules are in [India company basics](india_company_basics.md).

## 1. Is freelancing right for you now?

| You gain | You take on |
|---|---|
| Choice of clients and projects; direct link between work and income | Finding work (often 20–40% of your time), uneven income, no paid leave or employer PF |
| Broad experience across domains and stacks, fast | Invoicing, tax, contracts, collections |
| A path to consulting, productised services or a product | Isolation; less mentoring and code review than in a good team |

Freelancing straight out of college works best when you have **one clearly sellable skill** (for example, building Next.js front ends from Figma designs, setting up CI/CD and cloud infrastructure for small teams, or building data pipelines and dashboards) and evidence of it. Without that, a job first builds the judgement clients pay for. If you are employed, read your contract's outside-work and IP clauses first ([contract clauses](../07_job_search_offers_negotiation/offers_and_negotiation.md#5-contract-clauses-to-read-before-signing)).

## 2. Finding clients

**Channels, from strongest to weakest for most people (analysis):**

1. **Past employers, managers and colleagues.** They know your work. Tell them you are available and what you do.
2. **Referrals from happy clients.** Ask every satisfied client for one introduction.
3. **Communities** where your clients gather (startup founders' groups, local business associations, open-source project users).
4. **Public evidence:** case studies and posts on the problem you solve ([writing](../06_portfolio_resume_github/case_studies_and_writing.md)).
5. **Agencies and larger freelancers** who subcontract overflow work.
6. **Marketplaces.**

**Marketplaces (check current terms; fees change):**

| Platform | Model | Fee to freelancer |
|---|---|---|
| [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/) | Open marketplace; proposals bought with "Connects" | Reported to have moved from a tiered fee to a variable fee of 0–15% per contract from 1 May 2025 (third-party reports; confirm on Upwork's help centre) |
| [Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/) | Sellers list fixed-scope "gigs" | Commission on each order (check current rate) |
| [Freelancer.com](https://www.freelancer.com/) | Open marketplace with bidding | Check current rate |
| Vetted networks (for example [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/)) | Screening tests and interviews before you can take work | Rate set or negotiated through the network |
| [Contra](https://contra.com/) | Portfolio-based independent work | Check current terms |
| [Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/) | Startup jobs including contract roles | — |

Marketplace realities: early on, you compete on price against many others; reviews compound, so the first few jobs matter most; read the platform's rules on moving clients off the platform before you do it, since many prohibit it for a period.

## 3. Setting rates

**Start from what you need, not from what others charge.**

```text
Hourly rate = (target annual income + business costs + taxes + savings for gaps)
              ÷ billable hours per year
```

**Billable hours** are far fewer than working hours: finding clients, admin, learning and gaps between projects take time. A common planning assumption is that 50–65% of working time is billable (**analysis**, not a measured statistic; track your own).

**Illustrative calculation (India-based, foreign and Indian clients mixed):**

| Item | Annual (₹) |
|---|---|
| Target take-home income | 12,00,000 |
| Costs: laptop replacement, software, internet, co-working, accountant | 1,50,000 |
| Health insurance and a retirement contribution you would otherwise get from an employer | 1,00,000 |
| Income tax (estimate with a CA for your situation) | 1,50,000 |
| Buffer for gaps between projects (two months of expenses) | 2,00,000 |
| **Revenue needed** | **18,00,000** |
| Working weeks (52 − 6 for holidays and illness) | 46 |
| Billable hours (46 weeks × 40 hours × 55%) | ≈ 1,012 |
| **Minimum hourly rate** | **≈ ₹1,780** |

Then compare with the market for your skill and the value to the client. If the market pays much more for your niche, charge more. If it pays less, specialise further or reconsider.

**Pricing structures.**

| Structure | Use when | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Hourly or daily | Scope is unclear; maintenance; advisory | Clients watch hours; you earn less as you get faster (including with AI tools) |
| Fixed price per milestone | Scope is well defined and written | Scope creep; underestimation |
| Weekly sprint (fixed price per week of work) | Ongoing development with changing priorities | Needs clear weekly goals |
| Retainer | Ongoing availability (for example, 20 hours a month of support) | Unused hours disputes; define rollover |
| Value-based | You can quantify the outcome for the client | Needs trust and a clear measure |

**AI tools and pricing.** If AI tools make you twice as fast, hourly billing halves your income for the same outcome. Fixed-price milestones, sprints and value-based pricing let you keep the productivity gain, as long as quality and verification stay yours.

**Raising rates.** Raise for new clients first. Give existing clients notice (for example, 30–60 days) before a new rate applies.

## 4. Contracts

Every engagement needs a written agreement, even with friends. For small jobs, a master services agreement (MSA) plus a short statement of work (SOW) per project works well.

**Clause checklist.**

| Clause | What it should say |
|---|---|
| Parties | Legal names and addresses (your business name if you have one) |
| Scope and deliverables | Exactly what you deliver; what is **out of scope** |
| Acceptance | How the client accepts each deliverable and within how many days; silence after that period counts as acceptance |
| Timeline and dependencies | Dates, and what you need from the client (access, content, decisions) by when |
| Fees and payment | Amounts, currency, advance (many freelancers ask for 30–50% upfront for new clients; common practice), milestone payments, due dates, late-payment interest |
| Change requests | Changes are quoted and approved in writing before work starts (section 6) |
| Intellectual property | Who owns the code and when (usually: assigned to the client on full payment); your pre-existing tools and libraries remain yours, licensed to the client; open-source components keep their own licences |
| Confidentiality | Both sides protect confidential information |
| Data protection | If you handle personal data, what you may do with it, security duties, and deletion at the end ([DPDP](india_company_basics.md#8-data-protection)) |
| AI tools | Whether you may use AI tools, and that client confidential code or data is only shared with tools the client approves |
| Warranties | Limited (for example, fixing defects reported within 30 days); no guarantee of business results |
| Liability cap | Total liability limited, commonly to the fees paid under the contract |
| Termination | Notice period; payment for work done to date; handover |
| Independent contractor | You are not an employee; you control how the work is done |
| Governing law and disputes | Which country's or state's law; courts or arbitration (in India, under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996) |

**IP assignment under Indian law.** Under the Copyright Act, 1957, the author of a work made under a contract **for services** (a freelancer, unlike an employee under a contract **of** service) is usually the first owner, so the client needs a written assignment (section 19). The Act also fills gaps if the assignment omits terms: if no duration is stated it is deemed to be five years, and if no territory is stated it is deemed to be India. Assignments that are meant to be worldwide and permanent should say so explicitly. Confirm the wording with a lawyer.

**SOW snippet (illustrative).**

```markdown
## Statement of Work #3 · Order-tracking API
Deliverables: REST API for order status (3 endpoints, OpenAPI spec), Postgres schema and
migrations, tests (≥ 80% line coverage on the service package), deployment to the client's
AWS account via Terraform, README and runbook.
Out of scope: mobile app changes, admin dashboard, data migration from the old system.
Timeline: 3 weekly milestones starting 3 Nov 2026.
Fees: INR 2,40,000; 40% on signing, 30% at milestone 2, 30% on acceptance.
Acceptance: client reviews each milestone within 5 working days.
Change requests: quoted in writing; work starts after written approval.
```

## 5. Scoping and estimating

**Discovery questions before quoting:**

1. What problem are we solving, for whom, and how will we know it worked?
2. What exists today (code, systems, data, designs)? Can I see it before quoting?
3. Who decides, and who gives feedback?
4. What is the deadline, and what happens if it slips?
5. What is the budget range?
6. What must it integrate with? Who controls those systems?

**For unclear projects, sell a paid discovery phase first** (for example, one week producing a written plan, architecture and fixed quote). It protects both sides.

**Estimate with ranges.** For each task, estimate optimistic (O), most likely (M) and pessimistic (P) hours; the PERT estimate is (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6. Add the estimates, then add time for meetings, reviews, deployment and fixes (often 20–30% for small projects; track your own ratio). Quote fixed prices only for well-defined scope.

## 6. Avoiding scope creep

Scope creep is unpaid work that arrives as "small changes". Handle it the same way every time:

```text
Hi Neha, good idea. Adding CSV export to the order screen isn't in SOW #3, so I've written it
up as change request CR-2: about 6 hours, INR 12,000, and it would move the final milestone by
two days. Reply "approved" and I'll schedule it, or we can put it on the list for phase 2.
```

Habits that prevent it:

- Write "out of scope" lists in every SOW.
- Keep a shared **change log** with each request, estimate, decision and date.
- Never start unapproved work, even small items; small items add up.
- Offer a "phase 2" list for good ideas that would delay the current milestone.

## 7. Getting paid

- **Invoice promptly** at each milestone ([invoice fields](india_company_basics.md#6-invoicing)).
- **Advance payments** for new clients; release source code or production access on payment for the final milestone, as the contract allows.
- **Polite, scheduled follow-ups:** on the due date, at +7 days, at +14 days with late-fee terms, then pause work as the contract allows.
- **Indian business clients and MSME protection:** if you are registered as a micro or small enterprise under Udyam, the MSMED Act requires buyers to pay within the agreed period, not exceeding 45 days, or pay compound interest; delayed-payment complaints can be filed on the MSME Samadhaan portal ([India basics](india_company_basics.md#10-other-registrations-and-protections)).
- **Foreign clients:** see [receiving foreign payments](india_company_basics.md#7-receiving-foreign-payments).

## 8. From freelancer to consultant

Consultants are paid for judgement in a domain, not hours of typing. Moving up:

1. **Specialise** by problem and client type: "I help Indian D2C brands cut cloud costs" beats "full-stack developer".
2. **Productise** repeated work: a fixed-scope, fixed-price package ("Postgres performance audit: 5 days, report and fixes for the top 10 queries").
3. **Publish case studies** of results, with client permission.
4. **Offer retainers** for ongoing advisory and on-call support.
5. **Keep technical depth.** The value is in being right; keep building and reading ([staying future-proof](../09_staying_future_proof/README.md)).

## 9. Using AI tools in client work

- Follow the client's rules on which tools may see their code and data; put it in the contract.
- Review and test AI-generated code as carefully as code from a junior colleague; you are liable for what you deliver.
- Check licences: AI tools can reproduce licensed code; do not deliver code whose origin you cannot account for.
- Be honest if asked how you work. Clients pay for results and accountability, which stay with you.

## 10. Practice

1. Fill in the rate calculation with your own numbers and compare the result with three real listings for your skill.
2. Draft an MSA and one SOW for a realistic small project using the clause checklist; mark every clause you are unsure about as a question for a lawyer.
3. Role-play a scope-creep request with a friend and respond using the change-request template.
