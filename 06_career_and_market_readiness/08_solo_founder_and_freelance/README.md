AI coding tools have cut the cost of building software, so one person can now build what used to need a small team. They have not cut the cost of finding a real problem, reaching the people who have it, getting paid, and running a business legally. Those are what this folder teaches.

> **Not legal, tax or financial advice.** Pages that touch law and tax, especially [India company basics](india_company_basics.md), cite official sources and were checked in October 2026. Rules change; confirm with a chartered accountant (CA), company secretary (CS) or lawyer before acting.

## Which path, when

| Path | Good first step if you… | Main risk | Typical first milestone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Freelancing** | Have one sellable skill now and need income | Trading time for money with no growth; feast-and-famine cash flow | 3 paying clients and a written process |
| **Consulting** (specialised freelancing) | Have deep experience in one domain | Depending on one or two clients | One retainer client |
| **Productised service** (a fixed scope at a fixed price, for example "Postgres performance audit, ₹X, 5 days") | Have repeated the same freelance job several times | Scope creep | 5 sales of the same package |
| **Software product (micro-SaaS, tool, app)** | Have found a problem people already pay to solve badly | Building something nobody buys | 10 paying users |
| **Open-source-based business** | Maintain a project with real adoption | Cloud providers or forks capturing the value | Paid tier or support contract |
| **Venture-funded startup** | Have a large market and need capital to reach it | Dilution, pressure, high failure rate | Funding plus a clear growth metric |

Many people combine paths: freelancing pays the bills while a product grows, or a job continues while a side project is validated. Check your employment contract for outside-work and IP clauses first ([offers: contract clauses](../07_job_search_offers_negotiation/offers_and_negotiation.md#5-contract-clauses-to-read-before-signing)).

## The LCH502 founder track

| Week | Milestone | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Problem discovery: 20 interviews | Interview notes, a problem statement with quotes ([idea to MVP](idea_to_mvp.md#2-problem-discovery)) |
| 4–5 | Validation: a landing page, a pre-sale or a concierge test | Sign-ups, pre-orders or a paid pilot |
| 6–9 | MVP built and in users' hands | Deployed product, security checklist done ([verify](idea_to_mvp.md#5-what-you-must-verify-yourself)) |
| 10–11 | Pricing and first sales | Price page, payments working, first revenue or a documented "no" |
| 12–13 | Legal and operations set-up as needed | The right structure, invoices, privacy notice ([India basics](india_company_basics.md)) |
| 14 | Review | A written review: what worked, numbers, what you would do next |

A carefully documented failure passes the course. "Launched, 40 people tried it, 0 paid, here is what I learned about why" is valid evidence and a strong interview story. Building in silence and showing nobody is not.

## Founding and employability

Founder and freelance experience counts in job searches when you can show it: shipped product, users, revenue, decisions and their outcomes. Write it up the same way as any project ([case studies](../06_portfolio_resume_github/case_studies_and_writing.md)).
