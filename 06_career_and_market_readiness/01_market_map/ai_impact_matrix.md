**This whole table is analysis.** It combines measured evidence (linked in the last column) with judgement about tasks. The task columns describe what AI tools commonly do in this work as of October 2026; they are not measurements for each cell. The outlook column states a direction and a confidence level.

How the terms are used:

- **Most automated now**: AI usually produces an acceptable first result, and a person reviews or accepts it.
- **AI augments**: a person does the task faster or better with AI, and makes the decisions.
- **Still human-led**: AI may assist, but the judgement, accountability or physical work stays with people.
- **Confidence**: **High** means official projections and other sources agree; **Medium** means some direct evidence, or sources disagree; **Low** means little direct data, so the outlook is inferred.

Evidence used across rows: task-level AI usage ([Anthropic Economic Index](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-january-2026-report), [Claude Code study](https://www.anthropic.com/research/impact-software-development)); what developers use AI for ([Stack Overflow 2026](https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/)); reliability limits ([METR](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)); and the role-specific sources in the last column.

## Patterns across the table

These are **analysis**, drawn from the rows above.

1. **The same task type is automated everywhere.** Boilerplate, first drafts, summaries and code from complete specifications appear in almost every "most automated" cell. They are easy to check and easy to describe.
2. **Human-led work shares three features**: accountability (someone signs off), missing context (the customer, the business, the physical device), and failures that are expensive or irreversible.
3. **Lower rungs are most exposed.** The tasks in the "automated" column are the ones that used to train beginners. This matches the payroll evidence on young workers ([Canaries](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2025/12/CanariesintheCoalMine_Nov25.pdf)) and the low entry-level hiring growth ([HackerRank 2025](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)).
4. **Verification is the common growth skill.** Every family gains value from people who can check AI output: tests, evals, reviews, measurements. That is why every course in this curriculum has a "verify AI output" section.
5. **Families tied to physical systems, regulation or production operations** (embedded, networking architecture, SRE, security architecture) are the slowest to automate, because mistakes cost the most.

## How to use this table

- **Choosing a major:** look at the "still human-led" column. That is what you will be paid for in five years.
- **Planning a year of study:** practise the "automated" tasks without AI until you can judge them, then use AI for them.
- **Preparing for interviews:** the "augments" column is where interviewers increasingly test how you work with AI. See [AI-driven interviews](../04_ai_driven_interviews/).
