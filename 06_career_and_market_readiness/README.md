This section turns what you learned into a job offer, a client or a company. It is a system, not a pile of tips: each folder produces a piece of **evidence** (a solved-problem log, a design document, a shipped project, a public profile, a tracked pipeline, a signed offer), and each course in Years 3–5 asks for specific pieces of it.

The market in one paragraph (details and sources in the [market map](01_market_map/README.md)): AI coding tools are standard equipment, with 65.9% of developers in the 2026 Stack Overflow survey using AI coding assistants or agents at work ([Stack Overflow 2026](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data)). Entry level is where the pressure is: US software developers aged 22–25 were close to 20% below their late-2022 employment peak by September 2025 ([Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2025/12/CanariesintheCoalMine_Nov25.pdf)), and India's industry body expects FY27 hiring "to shift from volume to skill mix" ([NASSCOM 2026](https://nasscom.in/knowledge-center/publications/technology-sector-india-strategic-review-2026)). The practical answer is to build the things AI substitutes least, judgement built by doing real work, and to make that judgement visible.

## 3. The readiness system, semester by semester

Each semester adds evidence. If you are a college student using this alongside a 4-year degree, compress semesters 9–10 into your final year.

## 4. The 12-month job-hunt timeline

Count back from the date you want to start work. "M-12" means twelve months before that date. Hours are per week, on top of coursework.

| Month | Focus | Concrete outputs | Hours/week |
|---|---|---|---|
| M-12 | Target and baseline | Choose a role family and two backups. Read their role pages and the [AI impact matrix](01_market_map/ai_impact_matrix.md). Take one timed mock coding interview without AI to get a baseline. | 4 |
| M-11 | Gaps | List the gaps between your evidence and the role's "durable skills" on its role page. Plan which project and which course closes each gap. | 4 |
| M-10 | Fundamentals | DSA on a schedule ([02](02_dsa_for_hiring/)): patterns, not problem counts. One SQL and one debugging drill a week without AI. | 6 |
| M-9 | Flagship project | Build the [industry project](05_industry_projects/) for your major. Write the design document first. | 8 |
| M-8 | Open source and writing | One merged contribution a month. One technical post or case study a month ([guide](06_portfolio_resume_github/case_studies_and_writing.md)). | 6 |
| M-7 | Profiles | [Resume](06_portfolio_resume_github/resume.md) v1, [GitHub](06_portfolio_resume_github/github_profile.md), [LinkedIn](06_portfolio_resume_github/linkedin_and_personal_brand.md). Get two reviews from working engineers. | 4 |
| M-6 | Target list and network | A list of 60–100 target employers in three tiers ([search strategy](07_job_search_offers_negotiation/search_strategy.md)). Ten conversations with people in your target role. | 5 |
| M-5 | System design and AI loops | [03](03_system_design/) basics; [04](04_ai_driven_interviews/) simulations in both AI-allowed and AI-forbidden formats. | 6 |
| M-4 | Applications open | Start the [pipeline](07_job_search_offers_negotiation/applications_pipeline.md): referrals first, then direct applications, then boards. 10–15 tailored applications a week. | 8 |
| M-3 | Interviews | Mock interviews weekly. A one-page "story bank" of 8 project stories. Debrief every real interview in writing. | 8 |
| M-2 | Offers | [Evaluate offers](07_job_search_offers_negotiation/offers_and_negotiation.md) on total value and learning, negotiate, check the contract. | 6 |
| M-1 | Close and prepare | Accept in writing, resign or finish college formalities, complete background verification, write a [90-day plan](07_job_search_offers_negotiation/first_90_days.md). | 3 |

**Three variants.**

- **Indian campus placement.** Many colleges run final placements early in the final year, so the "applications open" month arrives sooner. Move M-6 to M-4 forward by three to four months and treat campus drives as one channel, not the only one. Keep the off-campus pipeline running in parallel, because campus offers can be delayed or withdrawn by employers (see [layoffs and setbacks](07_job_search_offers_negotiation/layoffs_and_setbacks.md)).
- **Off-campus and self-learners.** There is no fixed season. Run M-4 to M-2 continuously until you have an offer, and expect more applications per interview than a referred or campus candidate.
- **Global and remote roles.** Add work authorisation and time-zone overlap to your target list from M-6. Visa-sponsored roles have their own calendar and rules, covered in [search strategy](07_job_search_offers_negotiation/search_strategy.md#5-remote-and-global-roles).

## 5. Readiness gates

A gate is something you can show, not a percentage.

**Intern-ready (L2).** You pass when you can:

1. Solve a medium-difficulty array, string, hash-map or tree problem in 35 minutes without AI, explaining the complexity aloud.
2. Show three repositories that build from a clean clone with one documented command, with tests that run in CI.
3. Show at least one merged pull request in a project you do not own.
4. Explain one project for five minutes, including one thing that went wrong and how you found it.

**Job-ready (L3).** You pass when you can:

1. Pass a 45-minute coding round in both formats: AI forbidden, and AI allowed where you must catch and fix flaws in AI-generated code (the format [Canva describes](https://www.canva.dev/blog/engineering/yes-you-can-use-ai-in-our-interviews/)).
2. Lead a 45-minute design discussion for a system in your major and write it up as a two-page design document.
3. Show a flagship project with measured results (latency, cost, accuracy or users) and a written case study.
4. Show a one-page resume in which every bullet has evidence you can open in an interview.
5. Run a tracked application pipeline with weekly numbers.

## 7. Working with AI in your job hunt

- **Use AI for:** critiquing a resume draft against a job description, generating mock interview questions, role-playing a negotiation, summarising a company's engineering blog, checking a cover note for clarity.
- **Do without AI:** the first draft of anything that describes you, timed coding drills, and your own numbers. Some employers state this directly: Anthropic asks candidates to "create your first draft yourself, then use Claude to refine it", and to complete take-homes and live interviews without AI unless told otherwise ([Anthropic candidate guidance, updated 10 Jul 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance)).
- **Verify:** every claim, number and technology name an AI tool adds to your materials. If you cannot defend a line for five minutes in an interview, remove it.
- **Know each employer's rules:** some require AI in interviews and some forbid it (see the [market map, section 7](01_market_map/README.md#7-what-hiring-managers-now-screen-for)). Ask the recruiter which format applies, and practise both.
