> **Not legal or tax advice.** Rules on wages, tax and equity change, and individual situations differ. Figures here were checked in October 2026. Confirm anything that matters with your employer's HR or finance team, the official sources linked, or a chartered accountant or lawyer.

## 1. The parts of an offer

### 1.1 Indian CTC

"CTC" (cost to company) is everything the employer spends on you in a year, including money you do not receive monthly or at all in some years. Two offers with the same CTC can differ a lot in take-home pay.

| Component | What it is | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic salary** | The base on which PF and gratuity are calculated | What share of CTC is basic? |
| **HRA** (house rent allowance) | Allowance for rent; tax exemption only under the old tax regime | — |
| **Special allowance and others** | Balancing fixed components (LTA, meal cards, etc.) | Which are taxable under the regime you choose? |
| **Employer PF contribution** | 12% of PF wages paid into your provident fund ([EPFO](https://www.epfindia.gov.in/)). Many employers calculate it on the statutory wage ceiling of ₹15,000 a month (₹1,800 a month), others on full basic | Is PF on full basic or capped? It changes in-hand pay |
| **Gratuity** | Paid when you leave after qualifying service: 15 days' wages per completed year of service (15/26 of the last monthly wage × years). Employers often include an annual provision of about 4.81% of basic in CTC (15/26 ÷ 12) | You only receive it if you qualify; usually 5 years of continuous service for permanent employees. Fixed-term employees qualify after one year under the labour codes in force from 21 November 2025 ([DLA Piper, updated Feb 2026](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/government-of-india-notifies-the-labour-codes-ushers-a-new-era-of-compliances)) |
| **Variable / performance pay** | A target percentage paid if company and individual targets are met | Last two years' actual payout as a percentage of target; how ratings map to payout; is it pro-rated in the first year? |
| **Joining (sign-on) bonus** | One-time cash | Is there a clawback if you leave within 12 or 24 months? Is it paid in the first salary or after probation? |
| **Retention bonus** | Cash paid after you stay a set period | Conditions in writing |
| **ESOPs or RSUs** | Equity (section 3) | Number, vesting, value basis |
| **Insurance** | Group health, term life, accident cover | Is the premium included in CTC? Does it cover parents or spouse? |
| **Relocation** | Travel, temporary housing, shifting costs | Lump sum or reimbursed; clawback? |

**The labour codes and the "50% wages" rule.** India's four labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025, with some central and state rules still being finalised in early 2026 ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2025/government-of-india-notifies-the-labour-codes-ushers-a-new-era-of-compliances)). The Code on Wages, 2019 defines "wages" so that if excluded allowances exceed half of total remuneration, the excess is counted as wages. In practice many employers are restructuring pay so that basic (and similar components) is at least about half of fixed pay, which can raise PF and gratuity amounts and lower monthly take-home for the same CTC. Check the current status of the rules and how your employer applies them.

### 1.2 Global offers

| Component | Typical form | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Base salary | Annual, paid monthly or twice a month | — |
| Annual bonus | Target % of base | Historical payout |
| Equity | RSUs (public companies) or stock options (private companies) | Section 3 |
| Sign-on bonus | Cash, sometimes in two instalments | Clawback terms |
| Benefits | Health insurance, retirement match (for example, a US 401(k) match), leave | Cost to you; waiting periods |
| Visa and relocation | Sponsorship, legal fees, relocation package | Who pays; what happens if the visa is refused |

### 1.3 Terms that are not money

| Term | What to know |
|---|---|
| **Notice period** | Commonly 30 to 90 days in Indian tech employment (common practice, unconfirmed as a statistic); often shorter during probation. Some employers allow a "buyout" (you or your new employer pay salary in lieu of notice) or garden leave. A long notice period at your current job can cost you offers; ask new employers whether they will buy it out |
| **Probation** | Often 3–6 months, with a shorter notice period and confirmation at the end |
| **Background verification (BGV)** | With your consent, employers or their vendors check education, previous employment (dates, title, sometimes reason for leaving), address and sometimes criminal records. In India, your EPFO (UAN) history shows past employers. Discrepancies, even small ones such as wrong dates, can lead to withdrawal of the offer. Keep payslips, relieving letters and degree certificates ready |
| **Location and transfer** | Can you be moved to another city or client? |
| **Working hours and on-call** | Is on-call paid? How often? |

## 2. From CTC to monthly take-home: a worked example

**Illustrative scenario.** A ₹12,00,000 CTC offer in Bengaluru. Variable pay 10%. Basic is 50% of fixed. Tax under India's new regime using the rules for FY 2025-26 (standard deduction ₹75,000 for salaried employees; no tax on income up to ₹12 lakh after the rebate). The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, so check the current year's slabs with the [Income Tax Department's calculator](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/) before relying on these numbers.

| Line (annual, ₹ unless stated) | PF on capped wages (₹15,000/month) | PF at 12% of full basic |
|---|---|---|
| CTC | 12,00,000 | 12,00,000 |
| Variable (paid later, if earned) | 1,20,000 | 1,20,000 |
| Gratuity provision (4.81% of basic) | 24,845 | 23,948 |
| Employer PF | 21,600 | 59,776 |
| **Fixed gross pay** (CTC minus the three lines above) | **10,33,555** | **9,96,274** |
| Basic (50% of fixed) | 5,16,777 | 4,98,137 |
| Monthly fixed gross | 86,130 | 83,023 |
| Less employee PF (monthly) | 1,800 | 4,981 |
| Less professional tax (monthly; state-specific, Karnataka ≈ ₹200) | 200 | 200 |
| Less income tax (taxable ≈ ₹10.8 lakh and ₹10.4 lakh including full variable, both below ₹12 lakh) | 0 | 0 |
| **Approximate monthly in-hand** | **≈ ₹84,100** | **≈ ₹77,800** |

Because gratuity and PF depend on basic, and basic depends on fixed pay, the fixed figure is solved from: CTC = fixed + variable + employer PF + 0.0481 × basic.

What the example shows:

1. Monthly take-home is about **78–84% of CTC ÷ 12** here, and lower when PF is on full basic. The difference is not lost: it goes into your PF account, but you cannot spend it monthly.
2. **Variable pay is not monthly** and may pay less than 100%.
3. **Gratuity is in CTC but paid only if you qualify.** If you leave after two years as a permanent employee, you usually never receive it.
4. Above about ₹12 lakh of taxable income, tax starts to apply under the new regime and grows with slabs; at higher CTCs, tax and equity treatment dominate the comparison.

Build the same table for every offer before comparing. A spreadsheet with your own numbers takes 20 minutes.

## 3. Equity: ESOPs and RSUs

### 3.1 How they work

| | ESOPs (employee stock options) | RSUs (restricted stock units) |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | The right to **buy** shares later at a fixed **exercise (strike) price** | Shares delivered to you as they **vest**, for free |
| Typical at | Startups and private companies | Public companies (and some late-stage private ones) |
| Value | (Share value at exit − strike price) × options, if there is ever an exit | Share price × shares vested |
| Main risk | Shares may never become sellable; preference rights of investors are paid first in a sale | Share price falls |

**Vesting.** Equity is earned over time. A common pattern is four years with a one-year "cliff" (nothing vests before the first anniversary, then 25% vests, then monthly or quarterly), but schedules vary, including back-loaded ones. Read yours.

**Exercise window after leaving.** For options, you may have only a limited period after leaving to buy your vested options, or lose them. Windows vary from weeks to years by company. Ask.

**Liquidity.** Options in a private company are only worth money when you can sell: an acquisition, an IPO, a company buyback, or an approved secondary sale. Ask whether the company has ever run a buyback for employees.

### 3.2 Taxes in India (check current rules)

- **At exercise (ESOPs) or vesting (RSUs):** the difference between fair market value and the price you pay is taxed as a salary **perquisite**. For ESOPs this means you can owe tax when you buy shares you cannot yet sell.
- **At sale:** capital gains on the difference between the sale price and the fair market value used at exercise or vesting.
- **Eligible startups:** since the Finance Act 2020, employees of eligible DPIIT-recognised startups (with the income-tax exemption certificate) can defer the tax on the ESOP perquisite until the earliest of: 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year, leaving the company, or selling the shares. Confirm how this carries over under the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- **RSUs from a foreign parent company:** taxed in India as a perquisite at vesting if you are resident; foreign shares must be reported in the foreign-assets schedule of your income tax return. Non-disclosure can attract penalties.

### 3.3 Questions to ask about equity

1. How many options or units, and what percentage of fully diluted shares is that?
2. Strike price, and the latest fair-market-value (valuation) per share?
3. Vesting schedule, cliff, and any acceleration on acquisition?
4. Exercise window if I leave?
5. Has the company done employee buybacks? At what price?
6. (Startups) How much has been raised, what are the liquidation preferences, and how many months of runway?

**Valuing startup options.** Treat them as a small probability of a large outcome. A simple method: estimate a pessimistic (0), base and optimistic exit value per share, weight by your honest probabilities, subtract the strike price and tax, and compare with the cash you are giving up. Do not accept a lower salary for equity you could not afford to see go to zero.

## 4. Negotiation

### 4.1 Principles

1. **Negotiate after a written offer**, when the employer has decided they want you.
2. **Be specific and honest.** Never invent a competing offer or a current salary. Background checks and payslips reveal it, and offers are withdrawn for it.
3. **Use market data, read correctly.** [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) mostly reflects product companies and total compensation; [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/), AmbitionBox and Glassdoor reflect broader markets and often base pay. The [market map](../01_market_map/README.md#6-india) explains why the two India figures differ by almost four times.
4. **Negotiate the whole package:** fixed pay, joining bonus, equity, level, start date, notice buyout, team, remote days, learning budget.
5. **Stay warm.** You will work with these people. Enthusiasm plus a clear ask works better than pressure.

**Freshers and campus offers.** Mass-hiring and campus offers usually have fixed bands with little room to negotiate, and placement cells may have rules about accepting and declining offers. You can still ask clarifying questions (joining date, location, bond terms, variable pay) without negotiating the number.

### 4.2 "What is your current and expected CTC?"

Indian recruiters commonly ask both, and some anchor the offer on a percentage increase over current pay (common practice; unconfirmed as a statistic). Some jurisdictions forbid salary-history questions altogether; California, for example, bars employers from asking applicants about salary history ([California Labor Code §432.3](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=432.3)).

Scripts:

```text
Early in the process (expected CTC):
"I'm focusing on whether the role is a fit first. From what I've seen for backend roles at
this level in Bengaluru, I'd expect a total package in the range of ₹X to ₹Y, depending on the
structure. What's the budgeted range for this position?"

Current CTC (if you choose to share, share it accurately and completely):
"My current CTC is ₹A, of which ₹B is fixed. My expectation is based on the scope of this role
and the market for it rather than on my current salary, which reflects a services-company
band for a different role."
```

### 4.3 Responding to an offer

```text
"Thank you, I'm really excited about the team and the ledger work. Could you send the full
breakdown in writing: fixed components, variable target and last year's payout, PF basis,
joining bonus terms, and equity details? I'd like to review it and come back to you by
Thursday."
```

### 4.4 The counter

```text
"Thank you for the detailed offer. I'd like to make this work. Based on [the other offer I
have at ₹X fixed / market data for this level from <source>], is there flexibility to bring
the fixed pay to ₹Y? If we can get there, I'm ready to accept this week."
```

If fixed pay cannot move:

```text
"I understand the band is fixed. Could we look at a joining bonus of ₹Z to cover the
variable pay I'll forfeit by leaving before my company's payout in April, or a notice-period
buyout so I can join sooner?"
```

### 4.5 Deadlines and competing processes

```text
Exploding offer:
"I'm very interested. I'm in final rounds with one other company and expect to hear by the
20th. Could you extend the deadline to the 22nd so I can make a fully committed decision?"

Telling a company you have an offer (only if true):
"I've received an offer from another company with a deadline of the 18th. You're my first
choice; is there any way to accelerate the remaining rounds?"
```

### 4.6 Declining and accepting

- **Declining:** thank them, say you have chosen another role, keep the door open. Do not burn bridges; people move between companies.
- **Accepting:** in writing, after reading the final contract. Once you accept, stop interviewing elsewhere. Reneging on an accepted offer is legal in many situations but damages your reputation, and campus placement rules may penalise it.

## 5. Contract clauses to read before signing

| Clause | What to check |
|---|---|
| **Notice period and buyout** | Length during and after probation; whether the employer can refuse a buyout |
| **Non-compete** | In India, section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 makes agreements in restraint of a lawful profession, trade or business void to that extent; courts have generally not enforced post-employment non-competes, while confidentiality and non-solicitation clauses are more likely to be upheld. Read it anyway and ask for unreasonable terms to be removed |
| **Service agreement or training bond** | Amount, duration, and whether it is a reasonable pre-estimate of training costs. Courts look at reasonableness, and outcomes vary. **Do not hand over original certificates** as security; offer verified copies instead |
| **Intellectual property and side projects** | Under India's Copyright Act, 1957, work made in the course of employment under a contract of service generally belongs to the employer. Ask for a written carve-out for side projects and open-source work done on your own time and equipment |
| **Outside work (moonlighting)** | Whether freelancing or a second job needs permission. Undisclosed dual employment is a common reason for termination |
| **Clawbacks** | Joining bonus, relocation, training costs: amounts and periods |
| **Variable pay** | Whether it depends on being employed on the payout date |
| **Background verification consent** | What will be checked and by whom |
| **Data and AI tools** | Policies on using external AI tools with company code or data |

## 6. Comparing employers

**Analysis.** These are typical patterns, not rules; individual teams differ more than company types.

| Type | Pay pattern | Learning and scope | Stability | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large product company / big tech | High fixed plus RSUs | Strong engineering practices, narrow scope at first | Usually good, but large layoffs happen | Team matters more than brand; ask about the team's work |
| Global capability centre (India) | Often above services, below US big tech | Varies from core product work to support functions | Depends on the parent's strategy | Is the team building core systems or maintaining them? |
| Funded startup | Lower fixed, ESOPs | Broad ownership quickly, less mentoring | Depends on runway | Runway, revenue, founder track record |
| Bootstrapped or profitable small company | Moderate fixed, sometimes profit-sharing | Broad scope | Often steady | Growth prospects |
| IT services and consulting | Lower fixed for freshers; large structured training | Client-dependent; can include strong domain exposure | Historically high-volume hiring, now shifting to "skill mix" ([NASSCOM 2026](https://nasscom.in/knowledge-center/publications/technology-sector-india-strategic-review-2026)) | Project allocation, bench time, bonds |
| Research lab or university | Lower pay, sometimes stipends | Depth and publications | Grant-dependent | Supervisor fit |

**A decision matrix.** Weight what matters to you (weights sum to 100), score each offer 1–5, multiply and add.

| Criterion | Weight | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-year cash (from your take-home table) | 25 | 3 | 4 |
| Learning: mentorship, code review, scope | 25 | 5 | 3 |
| Work you would show in 2 years (portfolio value) | 20 | 4 | 3 |
| Stability (runway, business health) | 15 | 3 | 5 |
| Equity expected value | 5 | 4 | 1 |
| Location, hours, life | 10 | 3 | 4 |
| **Weighted total** (Σ weight × score ÷ 100) | 100 | **3.75** | **3.55** |

Early in a career, learning and the evidence you will be able to show often compound more than a small difference in first-year pay (**analysis**). But do not ignore cash if you have loans or family obligations.

**Questions for the team before you decide:**

1. What would I ship in my first three months?
2. How are code reviews done, and how long does a typical pull request wait?
3. How often do you deploy? What does on-call look like?
4. Which AI tools are allowed, and what are the rules for code and data?
5. Who would mentor me? How many people joined or left the team last year?
6. (Startups) How many months of runway? What happens if the next round is late?

## 7. After you accept

- Get the final offer letter and appointment letter; check every number against the breakdown.
- Resign in writing, serve or negotiate the notice period, and collect your **relieving letter**, **experience letter**, last payslips and **Form 16**.
- Link your PF account to the new employer through your UAN.
- Complete background verification promptly and accurately.
- Write a [90-day plan](first_90_days.md).
- If an offer is delayed or withdrawn, see [layoffs and setbacks](layoffs_and_setbacks.md#3-a-delayed-or-withdrawn-offer).

## 8. Practice

1. Take any offer letter (yours, a friend's with permission, or the illustrative one above) and build the CTC-to-in-hand table in a spreadsheet.
2. Role-play the counter (section 4.4) with a friend playing a recruiter who says "the band is fixed". Practise three non-salary asks.
3. Score two real or hypothetical offers with the decision matrix and write one paragraph explaining your choice.
